# Comparison framework

How to write honest, high-ranking comparison content. Comparison and "alternatives to X" pages are usually the **highest-converting** content an AI startup can publish, the reader is already in-market. They're also the easiest to get wrong (biased, dishonest, thin).

## The 3-competitor rule

The rule depends on the reader's question, not the page type:

- **Landscape content** ("what are my options", "best X tools", "alternatives to Y", "build vs buy"): name **at least 3** approved competitors. The reader doesn't know the field; fewer than 3 creates a false choice.
- **Head-to-head** ("{{PRODUCT}} vs CompetitorA"): cover the two named platforms in depth. Don't force-inject others. One sentence of "also consider Z if you need W" is enough, only if genuinely relevant.
- **Multi-way** ("{{PRODUCT}} vs A vs B"): cover all named; the rule is satisfied by the subject.

## Lead with the fork

Open comparison content by naming the **market fork** (from `foundation/market/positioning.md`). It frames the whole comparison honestly: "These tools split into camp 1 and camp 2. {{PRODUCT}} is camp 2; CompetitorA is camp 1. Here's what that means for you."

## Structure of a head-to-head

1. **One-paragraph verdict** up top (who each is for).
2. **The fork** — the architectural/philosophical split that sorts them.
3. **Comparison table** — dimensions that matter to the ICP, pulled from each competitor's fact pack. Only claim parity/advantage on features in `foundation/product/features.md`.
4. **When {{PRODUCT}} fits** / **When CompetitorA fits** — both honest.
5. **FAQ** — the real questions ("can I migrate", "what about pricing").

## Honesty rules (these make you rank and get trusted)

- **Acknowledge where the competitor wins.** Readers and search engines reward it.
- **No false dichotomies.** Never "build it yourself vs {{PRODUCT}}."
- **Position on differentiation, not superiority.**
- **No monopoly claims** without a dated source.
- **Facts from fact packs; opinion written fresh.** Pull competitor facts from `foundation/market/competitors/direct/<name>.md`; write your take on them here, in the article. Never store your opinion in the fact pack.

## Sourcing

Every factual claim about a competitor traces to its fact pack, which traces to a dated source URL. If a fact isn't sourced, don't publish it.
